Overwatch - Perk Keybinds Design Concept

Your perks, your order, no more panic-picks

  • Game/Engine: Overwatch
  • Role: Designer
  • Timeframe: August 2026

Design Intent

The addition of perks within Overwatch has provided players with an additional task to think about outside of team fights and individual tasks. With the amount of information players have to process before, during, and after fights, it can sometimes be too much to think about other gameplay features. Selecting the wrong perks suck. Hectic fights can cause players to select unintended perks, forcing them to play the rest of the match in a style they never intended. Flushing out the customizability for perks could alleviate the pressure of selecting the preferred perks by giving players the ability to customize the order in which perks are displayed as well as increasing transparancy on what perks are being selected.

Outcome & Reflection

Compared to other game design related changes I could've made, this project was on the easier side as it involved less consideration on whether change impacted players in a negative way. The idea doesn't seek to disrupt any player experiences like design changes sometimes do. Helping people select perks easier is something that could benefit all types of players regardless of skill level.

Even though this is a small, exploratory project, I think that the planning involved is something I'm really proud of just because of how streamlined it was. Identifying the core problem and then working through possible solutions to that problem is something that is, in my opinion, a vital component to any design-related process. There wasn't as much to planning compared to previous projects but planning out each idea and analyzing the strengths and weaknesses of each approach made the rest of the project a lot faster. Planning was a huge problem in previous projects so I'm glad that it's improved, even if that improvement isn't astronomical.

Being exposed to this type of design, where I'm primarily focused on user experience and designing user interfaces, was something that I enjoyed doing. Opens the perpsective on different kinds of accessibility settings and is something that I'll continue to think about while designing features for any game.
